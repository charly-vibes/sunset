* Purpose of this review: document-conformance requires the extractor-output
  projection to match observations "by resolved source anchors or an
  independently reviewed explicit mapping". The mapping below is

* What you are NOT reviewing: extraction quality. The corpus descriptors are

  the /comparison rules/, not their results.

## What to review (4 files, ~25 min)

1. =dsc03/corpus.py= (242 lines) — the 12 corpus cases.
   - *Check:* each case actually exercises the adversarial situation its name
     claims. The two easiest to fake are =shared-pages= (does one physical page
     really back two logical articles?) and =ocr-native= (is the OCR text
     really a plausible mis-read: "2l" vs "21"?).
   - *Check:* =conflicting-interpretations= records two INCOMPATIBLE supported
     hypotheses with no winner ("ambiguity can pass").
   - *Check:* the two carrier-derived cases (=epub-links=, =omission=) point at
     committed fixtures and the =omission= case really flips the last page's
     glyph entry to =omitted= with a reason.
2. =dsc03/projection.py= (157 lines) — the matching rules. This is the heart
   of the review.
   - *Rule 1 — anchor-only matching:* observations pair only when they share a
     non-empty anchor set AND the same observable. Local ids (=a7= vs =b2=) and
     text similarity NEVER cause a pairing.
   - *Rule 2 — ambiguity stays unresolved:* >1 candidate pairing, or a pairing
     whose values diverge, or same-observable/no-shared-anchor → =unresolved=
     with BOTH sides attributed. No arbitrary winner.
   - *Rule 3 — dropped-observation is not "unresolved":* if a required
     observable is simply absent from one extractor, that is a false negative,
     not an allowed unresolved alternative — even if the oracle lists the
     observable in =allowed_unresolved=.
   - *Rule 4 — pass rule:* pass iff every required observable is matched or
     actually-unresolved, unresolved ⊆ oracle's allowed list, nothing
     forbidden present.
   - *Check:* would you accept these four rules as the "pinned common
     projection and comparator"? Any rule you'd change is a finding.
3. =dsc03/canonical.py= (75 lines) — the two readers.
   - *Check:* reader_a (recursive sort-keys walk) and reader_b (top-level
     section emitter) are genuinely different code paths, and the output
     contract (ids, array order, exact non-NFC text) is honest.
4. =tests/tracer/test_conformance_corpus.py= (250 lines) — the encoded
   expectations.
   - *Check:* each projection test asserts the behavior you'd expect from the
     rules above; especially =test_label_similarity_never_matches_without_anchor=
     and =test_discarding_an_expected_alternative_fails=.

## Known limitations to weigh (please judge severity)

- The anchors are synthetic strings (=sha256:#page:1= style), not resolved
  against real source bytes; anchor /resolution/ against carrier digests is
  future work, matching is by anchor equality only.
- =dependencies= / =derived-from= semantics in the =replay= corpus case were
  built to satisfy the validator's lineage rules — check they read honestly.
- =allowed_unresolved= is a flat observable-name list; per-observation
  granularity would be stricter.
